# Speaking ISO 20022

Everything this project settles is a payment, and the rest of the industry has
one way of describing payments. A corridor that cannot emit a `pacs.008` needs
a translation layer written by whoever adopts it, and the translation is where
meaning gets lost.

`services/iso20022` maps ledger state to the standard shapes directly.

## The mapping

| Ledger state | Message | Status | What it says |
|---|---|---|---|
| CAPTURED | `pacs.008` | — | the instruction, as sent |
| ACCEPTED | `pacs.002` | `ACTC` | accepted, technically validated |
| PREPARED | `pacs.002` | `PDNG` | both legs reserved, nothing moved |
| SETTLED | `pacs.002` | `ACSC` | accepted, settlement completed |
| ABORTED | `pacs.002` | `RJCT` | rejected, with a reason |
| ABORTED after PREPARED | `pacs.004` | — | the return of what was reserved |

```bash
peer chaincode query -C mychannel -n settlement \
  -c '{"Args":["GetTransfer","TP-1"]}' | node services/iso20022/src/cli.js

./network/demo-iso20022.sh --out ./iso-messages
```

## Where the standard and the ledger disagree

### PREPARED has no counterpart

ISO has no status for *"both sides have irrevocably committed and neither has
moved"*. The standard grew up around rails where that state does not exist: a
payment is instructed, and then it has happened or it has not.

`PDNG` is the closest honest answer, and it **understates what the ledger
knows**. A prepared transfer is far more certain than a pending one — both
payers' funds have left their balances and sit in escrow, and the only
outcomes left are settle or refund.

That gap is the two-phase commit showing through the standard. It is recorded
rather than papered over, because a counterparty reading `PDNG` and treating
it as ordinary uncertainty would be underestimating its position.

### The messages are commercially incomplete, on purpose

`pacs.008` has rich debtor and creditor structures: name, postal address, date
and place of birth. **Every one of them is absent here**, because the ledger
holds a salted hash and nothing else.

What is emitted is the opaque reference in a proprietary identifier:

```xml
<Cdtr>
  <Id><PrvtId><Othr>
    <Id>sha256:collegefeespayeeref…</Id>
    <SchmeNm><Prtry>TRIBUTARY-SALTED-REF</Prtry></SchmeNm>
  </Othr></PrvtId></Id>
</Cdtr>
```

That is the correct output for a message built **from the ledger**. A real
sender would populate the personal fields from its own compliance vault on the
way out — never from the chain. Anything else would mean the names were on the
chain, which is the one thing this project will not do.

`no personal data field is emitted, because there is none to emit` is the test,
and it checks for the absence of `Nm`, `PstlAdr`, `BirthDt` and `EmailAdr`
rather than trusting that nobody adds one.

### Amounts are converted, not divided

The ledger holds 5003482 paise; ISO wants `50034.82`. The obvious conversion is
`amount / 100`, and it is wrong: a `uint64` of minor units exceeds what a
double holds exactly, so dividing turns an exact figure into an approximate one
at the last step before it leaves the system.

```
exact:  90071992547409.93
divide: 90071992547409.92
```

The conversion is string surgery on the decimal digits instead, and a caller
passing a JavaScript `number` is refused outright — by the time it is a number
the precision may already be gone.

**Not every currency has two decimals.** JPY has none and a divide-by-100 would
report a hundredth of the real amount; KWD has three and the same code would
report ten times too much. The table is explicit and an unknown currency is
refused rather than assumed.

### The rate may not have a decimal

The ledger holds a rate as an exact rational — `227431/10000`. ISO's
`XchgRate` is a decimal. For this corridor the two agree exactly, because the
denominator is a power of ten, but that is a property of this rate rather than
of rates in general.

A rational terminates in decimal exactly when its reduced denominator has no
prime factors besides 2 and 5. When it does not, the mapping rounds and **says
so** — the CLI prints a warning and the caller can refuse to emit rather than
quietly ship an approximation as if it were the rate.

## What has not been done

- **No XSD validation.** The official schemas are licensed and not in this
  repository, so messages are checked for well-formedness and for the elements
  that matter, not against the normative schema. A deployment that exchanges
  these with a real counterparty must validate against the real XSDs.
- **No `head.001` business application header.** Messages are emitted bare. A
  real exchange wraps them.
- **No `camt` reporting.** Account statements and notifications are a separate
  family and nothing here produces them.
- **Charges are stated, not modelled.** `ChrgBr` is `SHAR` by convention; the
  ledger models no charges at all, so that field states an assumption rather
  than reporting a fact.
- **Nothing consumes these.** They are produced and checked, never sent.
  Receiving a `pacs.008` and turning it into a transfer is the other half, and
  it is not built.
